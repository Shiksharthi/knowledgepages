Study Notes

Who they are

Walmart Global Tech is the technology arm of Walmart Inc. (NYSE: WMT) — the company behind the world's largest retailer. Their framing: a "people-led and tech-powered" organization where one line of code can improve life for hundreds of millions of people. Roots in Bentonville, Arkansas (Sam Walton opened the first store in 1962), with major tech hubs in Sunnyvale CA, Bellevue WA, Reston VA, and a large India Development Center.

Scale (FY2026)

Revenue >$700B eCommerce >$150B (+24%) Advertising +46% Membership fees >$4.3B Associates ~2.1M

The strategic story (say this)

Walmart has transformed from a brick-and-mortar discounter into a tech-driven omnichannel ecosystem — growth now comes from high-margin streams: digital advertising, marketplace, fulfillment services (WFS), and automated supply chain. By early 2026, ~65% of stores are automated for fulfillment. The next chapter is explicitly AI-driven — that's your north star for any innovation question.

Signals worth name-dropping

  • New EVP of AI Acceleration, Product & Design role created — AI is now a top-line org priority, not a side project.
  • Public work on agentic systems "built for control, trust and scale."
  • Bringing restaurant ordering & delivery into the core Walmart shopping experience (June 2026).
  • Supply chain diversifying from China toward India and Southeast Asia.

This role sits in Retail Services, the org spanning Payments through Walmart Business (Walmart's B2B arm). It leans on fulfillment centers, stores, and merchants to grow a newer, fast-expanding part of Walmart. Treat it as a startup inside the enterprise — the JD calls for an "intrapreneurial spirit" and rapid program execution.

The four focus areas (memorize these)

  • Business-friendly purchasing — buying capabilities built for organizations, not households.
  • Onboarding & marketing — acquiring and activating business customers.
  • Business customer support & operations — servicing B2B accounts at scale.
  • Business engagement & monetization — deepening usage and turning it into revenue.

Why it matters in the interview

Walmart Business is a deliberate bet to compete in B2B (think Amazon Business). Show you understand B2B buyers differ from consumers — approvals, spend controls, invoicing, multi-user accounts. If you have retail eCommerce, B2B, or fulfillment-tech experience, lead with it; the JD names it explicitly.

Note: this posting states immigration sponsorship is not included — good to be aware of, no need to raise it unless asked.

  • John Furner — President & CEO of Walmart Inc. (effective Feb 1, 2026). Started as an hourly associate in 1993; led Walmart U.S. and Sam's Club. His line: a "new retail era fueled by innovation and AI."
  • Doug McMillon — Retired as CEO Jan 31, 2026 after a decade; remains on the board. Led the digital/eCommerce transformation. (Good context, don't dwell.)
  • Suresh Kumar — EVP, Global CTO & Chief Development Officer. He runs Walmart Global Tech — your top of house.
  • Daniel Danker — EVP, AI Acceleration, Product & Design. The clearest signal of where investment is going.
  • John David Rainey — EVP & CFO. Frames everything in cost-to-serve and profitable growth.
  • Greg Penner — Chairman of the Board.

Tip: tie your answers to Furner's "innovation and AI" framing and Kumar's "high tech with high empathy." It shows you read the room above the role.

Straight from this posting: you'll "spearhead some of the most complex and challenging programs," interacting with executive leaders, boots on the ground, and everyone in between. Four responsibilities, four things they're really testing:

Usher large, complex projects through discovery & planning with business, product, and UX

They're testing whether you operate upstream — shaping fuzzy ideas into scoped programs, not just executing tickets. Bring a story of taking something ambiguous to a funded, planned program.

Coordinate across several engineering teams to build the right solutions mapped to business strategy

Testing cross-team orchestration + technical judgment. Show how you connected what got built back to a business goal — and killed or redirected work that didn't.

Build cross-functional program plans; manage risk at program & portfolio level; drive execution

Testing your core TPM craft at scale — RAID, dependencies, critical path, status that executives trust. Have metrics: how you improved predictability or de-risked a slip.

As a Principal, lead the TPMs and continuously improve processes & execution models

Testing leadership and standard-setting. Have a story where you raised the bar for a team of PMs/TPMs — a framework, a cadence, or a coaching win.

Thread to pull through every answer: intrapreneurial speed inside a huge enterprise. They want someone who moves fast without breaking the org.
